# Release Notes: 13 November 2026

## 1. What Is There, Though Not Always Seen

In this update peira estimates what a survey's detections leave out. A species present at a site may go unseen, and an animal near a transect may not be noticed. Repeated visits, captures and distances are the designs that tell the two apart.

- **Occupancy:** sites visited several times; the share occupied and the chance of detection where occupied, with covariates of sites and visits; each site's chance of being occupied given what was seen.
- **Abundance from repeated counts:** the N-mixture model, Poisson or negative binomial, with each site's posterior abundance and a check on its upper bound.
- **Closed populations:** Lincoln–Petersen and Chapman with their intervals, Schnabel, and the models M0 and Mt by maximum likelihood over N.
- **Distance sampling:** line and point transects, half-normal and hazard-rate detection with cosine adjustments, density with its variance, and the key chosen by AIC.
- **One structure.** Each design is Horvitz–Thompson weighting with an estimated inclusion probability; the theory note proves it.
- Held to brute-force sums of likelihoods, closed forms, and hundreds of simulated surveys whose estimates recover the truth.
