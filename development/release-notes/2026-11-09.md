# Release Notes: 9 November 2026

## 1. Stated-Choice Designs

peira can now design stated-choice experiments ([decision 0007](../decisions/0007-stated-choice-designs.md)): which alternatives to show people together, so that their answers say as much as possible about what they value. Give the attributes and their levels, and a guess at the values; peira searches for the choice sets that make the estimates most precise, and reports how good the design is. For the simplest designs it reaches the best that can be done, exactly.

## 2. Interference

peira can now estimate what a treatment does when it reaches beyond the units it is given to: a closed street sends walkers to the next, a vaccinated child protects a classmate, a low-emission zone cleans the air beyond its edge ([decision 0006](../decisions/0006-interference.md)).

- **Rings of exposure.** Give each unit's distance to each treatment, in any metric, and when each treatment began. peira finds each unit's ring in each period, when it first entered each ring, and the ring in which it was first reached.
- **The direct effect and the spillover.** A regression on the rings gives each ring's effect against the unexposed. A staggered estimate by ring compares units entering a ring with those not yet reached by any treatment, so that units already spilled over are never taken as untouched.
- **Synthetic control without contamination.** The donors never reached by any treatment are named, so that a spillover does not bias the synthetic unit.
- **An exact test of no spillovers** in a randomised experiment, valid in samples of any size.
- **Works better.** The staggered estimator takes the period from which a unit was first touched, so that it is a comparison only until then.

## 3. Quasi-Experiments, Causal Graphs and Cohorts

peira's second stage ([decision 0004](../decisions/0004-quasi-experiments-and-causal-graphs.md)) adds what can be learnt when the mechanism of assignment is not known.

- **Treatments adopted at different times.** Estimate each group's effect in each period against units not (yet) treated, and their average by exposure (Callaway and Sant'Anna), where the usual two-way regression can mislead. The two-way regression and event study are kept beside them, for comparison.
- **Instruments.** Two-stage least squares, with its first-stage and overidentification tests, moved here from nomos; and the Wald estimator of the effect on compliers.
- **Cut-offs.** Regression discontinuity, sharp and fuzzy, with a bandwidth chosen by cross-validation.
- **One treated unit.** Synthetic control, with placebo inference across the donors.
- **Causal graphs.** Which variables a graph says are independent, and which sets identify an effect by adjustment (the back door) or through a mediator (the front door).
- **Cohorts.** Person-time and events on the Lexis diagram, rates with exact intervals, and direct and indirect standardisation: the beginning of the third stage, on which the new demography domain stands.
- Differences in differences moved from nomos with their checks, and the record of contributors now names the datasets the checks carry. 57 checks more, each against an exact truth.
