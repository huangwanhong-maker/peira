# Release Notes: 8 November 2026

## 1. peira Founded: Designs and the Mechanisms of Observation

peira is a new foundation for the methods every experimental and observational science shares: how units come to be treated and observed, and what can be learnt through that ([decision 0002](../decisions/0002-scope-boundaries-and-dependencies.md)).

- **Experiments.** Assign units by coin flips, a fixed number treated, blocks or clusters, with reproducible draws. Work out the power of a design and the sample it needs. Estimate an average effect by Neyman's difference in means, with its conservative variance. Test a sharp null exactly by Fisher's randomisation test.
- **Weighting.** Estimate totals and means by the inverse of the probability of being seen, Horvitz and Thompson's or Hájek's, with unbiased variance estimators. The same weights estimate treatment effects, and the doubly robust estimator stays right when either the propensity or the outcome model is.
- **Surveys.** Estimate totals, means and ratios from samples drawn by strata and clusters, with the standard errors survey practice uses, and rake the weights to known margins.
- **Survival.** Kaplan–Meier curves with Greenwood's errors and intervals, Nelson–Aalen hazards, the log-rank test for any number of groups, Cox's model with Breslow's or Efron's ties, and cumulative incidence with competing causes. All agree with statsmodels and lifelines on Rossi's recidivism study to about 10⁻¹⁴.
- **Missing data.** Pool multiple imputations by Rubin's rules with small-sample degrees of freedom, and impute within classes by the approximate Bayesian bootstrap.
- **Checked by the design itself.** Wherever a design is small enough, every possible assignment or sample is enumerated, so unbiasedness, variance formulas and a test's level hold exactly. A theory note gives the one structure behind it all, full data hidden in part by a mechanism, with proofs. 89 checks.
